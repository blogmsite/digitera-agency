ChatGPT Now Lets You Claim Your Business Listing — Do It Before Black Friday

By DigiTera • Published 12 November 2026 • 9-minute read • Filed under AI search & local SEO, Google Business Profile

On 10 November 2026, OpenAI quietly opened something small businesses have been waiting for: a way to claim the listing ChatGPT shows for your business. At the same time, Google finished a spam update that is quietly cutting the phone calls from Google Maps, and AI shopping assistants are about to decide a big slice of the Black Friday sale. Here is what changed, how to claim your listing in an afternoon, and the three other fixes to make before 27 November.

What actually changed on 10 November 2026

Until this week, ChatGPT's picture of your business was something it assembled from the web — your website, your reviews, directory listings, and whatever else it could find. You had no way to correct it.

That changed on 10 November 2026. A business can now claim the listing ChatGPT shows for it, in OpenAI's Business Hub. US local listings and online businesses are eligible first. The profile does not take reviews or posts, and claiming it does not change ChatGPT's recommendations — but it does let you correct your name, hours and website, and see how often ChatGPT mentions you.

That last part is the one to pay attention to. For the first time, you can measure whether the world's most-used AI assistant is even talking about your business.

Why does this matter now, and not next year? Because of the calendar. Black Friday is 27 November 2026 — fifteen days away. Adobe's September 2026 data shows visitors sent by AI assistants generate 83% more revenue per visit than other visitors, and Adobe forecasts AI traffic up 95% on Black Friday itself. Nearly a quarter of US online shoppers plan to begin their holiday shopping on an AI platform this year, up from 17% last year. The shoppers asking AI where to buy are the ones most likely to spend — and from this week, you can make sure the AI has your correct details when it answers.

How to claim your business on ChatGPT (the steps)

The claim process is short. Set aside an afternoon, and do it in this order:

1. Find your listing. Open ChatGPT and ask it about your business the way a customer would — "best [your service] near [your area]" or "[your business type] in [your town]". See whether your business appears, and what details ChatGPT gives. Note anything wrong: old hours, a wrong phone number, a dead website link.

2. Claim it in the Business Hub. OpenAI's Business Hub is where the claim happens. You will need to prove you own or manage the business — expect the same kind of verification other listing platforms use, such as confirming access to the business email or website. US local listings and online businesses are eligible first; if your market is not eligible yet, note it and check back weekly, because the rollout is moving fast.

3. Correct the basics. Fix your business name, hours and website first. These are the facts ChatGPT repeats when it recommends you, and they are the facts a wrong answer costs you a customer over. Then check the rest of the profile for anything stale — old services, a moved address, a closed branch.

4. Check how often ChatGPT mentions you. The Business Hub shows how often ChatGPT mentions your business. That number is your baseline. If it is zero, your job is not the claim — it is the visibility work in the next section. If it is growing, you now have a number to protect.

5. Keep it current. Treat the listing like your Google Business Profile, not like a one-off form. Hours change for the holidays, services change, and every change should be reflected in the hub the same week.

The bigger question: how do I get ChatGPT to recommend my business?

Claiming the listing is step one. Getting recommended is step two, and it is a different job. ChatGPT does not sell recommendations — its product results are selected independently and are not ads. What it does is read the web and pick the businesses it can describe confidently. That means the work is making your business easy to describe.

Be findable in the places AI reads. When a customer asks ChatGPT for a local business, it returns a short curated list — usually two or three names. It does not rely on Google's database alone. It reads Yelp, Bing Places, business directories and review sites. If your business is missing or wrong on those, you are invisible to the recommendation, however good your own website is.

Keep a clean product feed. For shops, this is now the deciding factor. ChatGPT's shopping picks increasingly come from merchant feeds — one vendor measured the share jumping from about 8% to about 65% during 2026. A free Google Merchant Center feed, accurate prices, in-stock status and real photos are the entry ticket. A study of about 5,000 ChatGPT product carousels found over 83% of the products closely matched Google's top 40 organic Shopping results for the same search — so the same feed work serves both Google and ChatGPT.

Collect real reviews, on and off your site. Reviews are the evidence AI uses to judge whether a business is trustworthy. That matters more than ever this month, because of the Google change in the next section.

Answer the questions customers actually ask, in plain words on your own site. AI answers are built from clear, extractable answers. Short direct-answer blocks near the top of a page, headings written as real questions, and an FAQ section on every service page all make your site easier for an AI to quote — and easier for a human to read.

How to get AI shopping assistants to recommend your products before Black Friday

The same logic applies to products, and the deadline is the same: Black Friday, 27 November.

AI shopping agents turn a request like "running shoes under $120 for flat feet" into a shortlist from product feeds, compare price, stock, reviews and policies, and then send the buyer to your checkout — or complete the order through a protocol where you stay the seller. Three things changed in the last few weeks:

  • Google's Universal Commerce Protocol checkout reached Canadian AI Mode and Gemini on 5 November 2026, the first deployment outside the US. Google's agents draw on a Shopping Graph of more than 60 billion listings.
  • Amazon opened its free Shop Direct Merchant Workspace on 4 November 2026, connecting any eligible retailer's own catalogue to Amazon and Alexa for Shopping at no cost.
  • Shopify's Fall '26 Edition (late October) lets shoppers search and buy from Shopify stores inside AI assistants like ChatGPT and Claude, paying with Shop Pay.

What to do this week, if you sell products:

  1. List your products free in Google Merchant Center with clean titles, prices and photos, and keep the feed current. A stale feed can block an AI transaction outright.
  2. Let AI crawlers reach your site. Blocking ChatGPT's crawler means ChatGPT cannot recommend you. Check your robots file before you blame the algorithm.
  3. Fix your product pages. AI assistants can only buy what structured, accurate data lets them buy. Full descriptions, real reviews, clear delivery and returns information.
  4. Do not wait for checkout integrations. Unless you are on Shopify, where they are already on, focus on being discovered first. Most of your buyers this November will still tap a link and decide on their phone in a few seconds.

Meanwhile on Google: the November spam update is cutting Maps calls

While AI search was opening up, Google was tightening the old one. Google's November 2026 spam update finished rolling out on 2 November — the third major spam update of 2026, after June and August. It targeted fake reviews, keyword stuffing in business names and profile descriptions, and low-quality third-party local citations.

Here is the part that should worry a legitimate business: Search Engine Land reported on 21 October that calls and clicks across Google Maps are falling even as map visibility holds steady. AI Overviews now answer many local queries directly, so fewer people need to click through or dial. Maps is becoming a place where people browse and compare — not a launch pad from which they call you.

The businesses still earning calls have three things in common: deep, structured content on their websites, a clean and actively managed Google Business Profile, and consistent signals across every directory where they appear.

Do this before the end of November:

  • Check your profile for spam-update damage. If your listing was propped up by bought reviews, a keyword-stuffed name or bulk citations — even through a vendor you stopped using years ago — the update may have trimmed your visibility in the local 3-pack. Clean it up: real name, accurate categories, genuine reviews only.
  • Answer the 10 questions customers ask most — on your website and in your profile description and services. Public Q&A is gone from Maps; since 12 March 2026, customers ask through Ask Maps, Gemini's conversational search, and get answers drawn from your content and reviews.
  • Mind the 4-day rule. Since September 2026, when Google suggests an edit to your profile, you have four days to accept or reject it. Miss it and Google decides.
  • Set your holiday hours now in your Google Business Profile, including Thanksgiving and the Friday after, so a customer never arrives to a closed door.
  • Use Gemini to manage the profile. Google's 5 November Small Business Bulletin confirmed Gemini Business Profile management is expanding to Google Workspace accounts, with direct photo and video uploads. It is still limited to owners managing a single verified profile — but for a one-location business, it is the fastest way to keep the listing fresh.

Your Black Friday AI-search checklist

Fifteen days is enough, if you start with the items that matter:

  • 1. Claim your ChatGPT Business Hub listing — and correct your name, hours and website.
  • 2. Update your Google Business Profile — holiday hours, services, fresh photos, and reply to your recent reviews.
  • 3. Check your product feed in Google Merchant Center — prices, stock, photos — and make sure AI crawlers can reach your site.
  • 4. Ask for genuine reviews from this week's happy customers. Do not buy them; the November spam update is built to find them.
  • 5. Write one clear answer on your site to the question your best customers ask most, in plain words, near the top of the page.
  • 6. Send three emails, not thirty — a teaser a week out, the launch on the morning the offer opens, and a last call to people who did not buy. Email and SMS drove 42% of Black Friday–Cyber Monday revenue for Klaviyo brands in 2025.
