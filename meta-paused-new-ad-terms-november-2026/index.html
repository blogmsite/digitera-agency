Meta Paused Its New Ad Terms (November 2026): What Small Businesses Must Do Before Black Friday

By DigiTera • Published 16 November 2026 • 11-minute read • Filed under Meta & Google ads, Black Friday

If you run ads for a small business, this has been a strange week. On Wednesday 12 November, Meta switched on a new way of counting conversions, and your reported purchases went up without a single extra sale. On Sunday 15 November — yesterday — Google started showing ads inside AI Mode in Pakistan and nine other countries, whether you asked for it or not. And on Friday 20 November, Meta's new ad terms were supposed to take effect, making you "solely responsible" for whatever its AI does to your ads. Except that last one is not happening any more. Meta has paused its new business and ad terms. The 20 November deadline is off, the old terms stay in force, and nobody has said when — or whether — the new ones will come back. If you run Meta ads, you need to know what changed, what did not, and what to do about the two changes that are still coming. Black Friday is 11 days away, and the auction is already a third more expensive than last year.

Change 1: Meta paused its new ad terms — the 20 November deadline is off

On 10 November 2026, Search Engine Journal reported that Meta had paused its updated Commercial Terms, Product Catalog Terms, Self-Serve Ad Terms and Ad Creative Generative AI Terms. The notice on Meta's Business Help Center says the updates "are now paused and will not take effect on November 20, 2026."

A Meta spokesperson told Nieman Lab on 5 November: "We're pausing the changes as we continue to engage with stakeholders and take our time to get this right."

The background matters, because it explains why this is a pause and not a withdrawal. Meta had planned to move the effective date from 30 October to 20 November after pushback from publishers and advertisers. Then an Oslo court temporarily blocked the terms on 28 October, and the UK Competition and Markets Authority opened an investigation on 30 October. Courts in Brazil and Norway have since entered preliminary injunctions against the terms. The CMA's investigation is still open, with its first phase of fact-gathering scheduled to run through January 2027. As of 10 November, Meta had set no new timeline.

What was supposed to change

Two clauses worried advertisers most.

First, an AI Features clause. The new terms said Meta's AI features "may make mistakes" and that you are "solely responsible for and assume any and all risks" of those features and the ad creative they produce — "even when you use our automated features." The current terms describe Meta's automated features as optional, something you may choose to opt in to or out of. The new version dropped that sentence. In plain words: if Advantage+ rewrites your ad and the rewrite is wrong, the bill — and the blame — would have been yours.

Second, a crawl clause. The new Commercial Terms would have let Meta, or anyone acting for it, crawl and cache domains and pages "associated with" a business's products or services, and create test credentials to reach those pages. The stated purpose was "advertising and business products."

What actually applies now

Here is the part most coverage skips: pausing the new terms does not mean Meta cannot crawl your site. The terms already in force let it do that.

  • The Self-Serve Ad Terms in force, dated 22 August 2025, already include a crawling grant. Placing an ad order lets Meta crawl advertiser websites, including for training — and the grant does not mention robots.txt.
  • The Ad Creative Generative AI Terms, dated 6 May 2024, also let Meta crawl URLs that advertisers provide, for use with their ads.
  • The Commercial Terms currently in force, dated 6 March 2024, do not include a crawling paragraph. The paused version would have added one covering sites associated with a business's products or services.

So the pause removes the new AI-liability wording and the broader crawl clause. It does not remove the crawling that already happens under the ad terms you accepted when you placed your last order.

What to do this week

  1. Do not panic, and do not celebrate. The terms are paused, not withdrawn. They may come back in a different form. Check Meta's Commercial Terms page directly before acting on any headline — the date on the page is the truth, not a blog post (including this one).
  2. Review your Advantage+ creative enhancements anyway. The liability clause is paused, but the AI is still editing ads. Meta's Help Center says some enhancements may be turned on by default, but you can turn them off at any time. Spend 20 minutes per ad account: open Ads Manager, look at what the automated features have changed in your live ads, and switch off anything you would not have approved yourself.
  3. Check your placements. Meta is gradually removing manual ad-set placement selection for Sales, Leads and App promotion campaigns. Once the change reaches your account, placements are automatic for those objectives. Awareness and Traffic campaigns are not affected. If your ads are suddenly appearing somewhere you did not choose, this is probably why — not a bug.
  4. Keep the receipt. If you run ads for clients, note the pause and the date in your own records. "The terms were paused on 5 November" is the kind of fact a client asks about three months later.

Change 2: Google AI Mode ads landed in Pakistan yesterday, 15 November

While Meta's change was being paused, Google's is going ahead. On 15 November 2026, ads inside AI Mode expanded to ten new English-language countries: Australia, Canada, India, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines and Singapore. Brazil, Mexico and the United Kingdom are planned for later in the fourth quarter.

Three things matter for a small business in Pakistan:

First, Pakistan supports text ads only, according to Google's availability documentation as relayed by Search Engine Journal. Shopping ads and the richer formats are not part of the Pakistan rollout yet.

Second, you do not need to do anything — and you cannot opt out. If you run Search, Shopping or Performance Max campaigns, they are automatically eligible for AI Mode placements. There is no separate campaign type, no separate budget line, no toggle to switch on, and no way to opt out of AI Mode specifically. Your ads have started appearing inside conversational AI answers.

Third, you cannot see the results separately. AI Mode impressions, clicks, conversions and costs are folded into standard campaign reporting. There is no AI Mode line in Google Ads, and reported search terms for AI Mode may only approximate the user's intent. You will not be able to calculate an AI Mode cost per acquisition — at least not yet.

AI Mode queries are also different from the searches you are used to. They tend to be longer and more conversational — "which plumber near me can fix a leaking tap today and how much will it cost" rather than "plumber near me." Google's own May 2026 report said follow-up questions in AI Mode were already rising by more than 40% a month on average in the United States, before this expansion.

What to do this week

  1. Watch your search-terms report after 15 November. Look for longer, question-shaped queries appearing in your campaigns. If they are converting, write ad copy and landing-page copy that answers them in plain language. If they are wasting spend, add negative keywords — but remember the reported terms may only approximate intent, so do not over-react to a single day.
  2. Check your landing pages answer questions, not just keywords. AI Mode rewards pages that answer a whole question clearly. A page that says "we fix leaking taps in Lahore, same-day service, call now" will do better than a page stuffed with the words "plumber Lahore" and nothing else.
  3. Do not build a separate AI Mode budget yet. There is nothing to build — the placements come from your existing campaigns. The work is in the copy and the landing page, not in a new campaign.

And one honest note: DigiTera wrote about this three days ago, when it was the third story of the week. It is the second story today because the launch was yesterday. If you are a Pakistani business and nobody has told you your ads have started appearing inside AI answers, that is the gap this post exists to fill.

Change 3: Meta ad costs are up 34.6% heading into Black Friday

The third change is not a policy. It is a price.

According to ad-tracking firm Birch, reported by CCO Ventures on 6 November 2026, US-targeted Facebook CPMs hit $21.45 in October 2026, up from $15.93 in October 2025 — a 34.6% jump. Meta's own Q3 2026 earnings show price-per-ad up 14% overall and up 23% in the US and Canada alone.

That is the auction you are about to bid in. Black Friday is Friday 27 November, Cyber Monday is Monday 30 November, and the five-day Cyber Week runs 26–30 November. In 2025, Cyber Monday outsold Black Friday online in the US — $14.25 billion against $11.8 billion, according to Adobe — and 16.6% of brands generated 30% or more of their entire year's orders in a single week (Omnisend).

The honest response to a 34.6% price rise is not "spend more." It is to make the spend you already have work harder, and to lean on the channels you own.

Email and SMS drove 42% of Black Friday Cyber Monday revenue across 10,000 Klaviyo brands in 2025. Flows — the automated emails that go out after a signup, an abandoned cart or a purchase — were only 5.3% of email sends and nearly 41% of email revenue. If your list is warm and your flows are working, they will do more for you this Black Friday than an extra hundred dollars in the auction.

And the discount itself deserves a calculation, not a guess. Set it from your break-even return on ad spend first: work out what a sale can cost you, then choose a discount that leaves room for it. Brands offering the smallest discounts grew fastest in 2025, according to Klaviyo. A smaller discount to your own list beats a bigger discount to cold traffic at a 34.6% higher CPM.

What to do this week

  1. Warm up your list now, not on 26 November. One useful email this week, one next week, then the offer. A cold list mailed for the first time on Black Friday is the most expensive way to sell.
  2. Open early access for past buyers on 23 November. Your best customers get first pick, and they convert at a fraction of the cost of cold traffic.
  3. Set the discount from the maths, not from the competition. Work out break-even ROAS, then choose the offer. If the numbers do not work at a 30% discount, do not offer 30%.
  4. Record your Meta baseline today. Meta's on-device conversion measurement switched on by default on 12 November, and reported purchases will rise without extra sales. Write down your real orders next to Meta's reported purchases now, so you can tell the difference when Black Friday numbers arrive.

The 20-minute checklist before Black Friday

Do these four things this week, in this order:

  • 1. Record your Meta baseline. Real orders vs Meta's reported purchases, side by side, starting today.
  • 2. Review Advantage+ creative. Twenty minutes per ad account; switch off any automated enhancement you would not have approved.
  • 3. Check your Google Ads search terms after 15 November. Look for long, conversational AI Mode queries; answer them on your landing pages.
  • 4. Warm up your email list. One useful email this week, one next week, early access for past buyers on 23 November.

None of this needs a new tool, a new budget or a new account. It needs twenty minutes and a decision.

The bottom line

Three ad changes landed in one week. One was paused, one launched yesterday, and one is a price rise you cannot pause at all.

Meta's new terms are off the table for now — but the terms already in force still let Meta crawl your site, and the AI is still editing your ads. Google's AI Mode ads arrived in Pakistan yesterday, text-only, with no opt-out and no separate reporting. And Meta's auction is 34.6% more expensive than it was a year ago, 11 days before the biggest shopping week of the year.

The businesses that do well this Black Friday will not be the ones that spent the most. They will be the ones that checked what changed, fixed what they could, and sold to the customers they already have.
